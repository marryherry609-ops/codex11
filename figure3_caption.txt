Figure 3. Gut–pulmonary vascular–right ventricular interactions and potential feedback in PAH.
Gut microbial compositional and functional changes and intestinal barrier impairment may alter systemic exposure to microbiota-related signals. The barrier-to-MAMP route indicates a possible increase in microbial-product exposure; it does not imply that all MAMP uptake requires barrier disruption. Metabolite exposure reflects microbial function together with host absorption, transformation and clearance. The two signal classes may modulate pulmonary vascular disease, but their complete causal paths in human PAH remain unvalidated. No uniform concentration increase or decrease is assigned to either signal class.
Pulmonary vascular remodeling and increased pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) can increase right ventricular (RV) afterload. RV adaptation and maladaptive remodeling are distinct, stage-dependent responses; hypertrophy does not necessarily imply decompensated RV function. The blue right ventricular cavity identifies the RV schematically rather than depicting measured remodeling. The afterload arrow terminates inside this cavity, separately from the RV label.
With RV dysfunction and systemic hemodynamic compromise, venous congestion and reduced intestinal perfusion may contribute to gut wall edema, impaired mucosal perfusion and barrier or local-milieu changes. Supporting human evidence for these reverse links derives principally from heart failure populations: intestinal congestion and bowel-wall abnormalities have been associated with RV dysfunction [68], reduced intestinal arterial blood flow has been observed in chronic heart failure [69], and increased intestinal permeability has been associated with higher right atrial pressure in advanced decompensated heart failure [70]. These observations do not establish the complete reverse causal sequence or feedback loop in human PAH. The shared source of the two dashed return routes denotes RV dysfunction and systemic hemodynamic compromise. The routes converge on intestinal alterations and then branch to barrier impairment and microbial compositional or functional shifts. The latter branch is a candidate ecological response to altered local conditions, rather than a demonstrated hemodynamic-to-microbiome causal sequence in human PAH. The heart-failure evidence annotation applies to the reverse gut links, not to the separate GG intervention study.
The separate experimental evidence strip summarizes Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus GG supplementation in monocrotaline-induced pulmonary hypertension (MCT-PH) rats. RV systolic and diastolic function improved and RV fibrosis decreased, whereas no significant between-group differences were detected in pulmonary vascular load or pulmonary arteriolar remodeling [71]. This finding supports evaluating pulmonary vascular and RV outcomes separately. It does not establish a direct cardiac action of a specific gut-derived molecule, and failure to detect between-group differences does not exclude pulmonary vascular effects.
The bowel illustration represents the intestine schematically. The adjacent, separately labeled Gut microbiota icon is a conceptual microbial community and is not placed in the central space enclosed by the colonic outline. The separate lower-left schematic depicts small-intestinal mucosa and is not a local anatomical magnification of the colon. Edema and reduced perfusion are represented by the pathway labels; the mucosal illustration is a general structural schematic.
Colors identify pathway classes: teal denotes microbiota-related signaling and navy denotes hemodynamic effects. Solid arrows denote the established afterload relationship. Dashed arrows denote proposed PAH connections, extrapolated reverse links or incomplete pathways. The thin lung–arteriole leader is anchored to a red intrapulmonary vascular branch, denotes anatomical localization and carries no causal direction. Evidence supports selected links; the complete feedback loop remains unvalidated.
Abbreviations: MAMP, microbe-associated molecular pattern; MCT, monocrotaline; PAH, pulmonary arterial hypertension; PH, pulmonary hypertension; PVR, pulmonary vascular resistance; RV, right ventricle/right ventricular.
